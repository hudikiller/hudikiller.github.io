CURE-Bench @ NeurIPS 2025

2025

CURE-Bench is a benchmark and competition for evaluating AI systems in clinically grounded drug decision-making and treatment planning, including treatment recommendation, drug safety and efficacy assessment, treatment design, and drug repurposing for diseases with limited therapeutic options.

My main contribution was the construction of the knowledge base for the proposed AgentKB framework. Following the paper's Reason-Retrieve-Refine (3R) pipeline, I adapted the student model to parse structured fields such as question, answer, prediction, and drug-infos, and generated intermediate outputs using the prompting strategy described in the paper.

Based on the student-stage outputs, I then used the teacher model to integrate question, answer, and reasoning fields together with the student's results, producing structured knowledge entries containing agent-plan and agent-experience. These entries formed the final knowledge base used by the agent system for cross-task retrieval and plan refinement.

OpenWebUI Preference Collection and Preference-Driven LLM Routing

2025

This project focused on secondary development of OpenWebUI for preference data collection and preference-driven model routing, with my main work centered on the design of an LLM router for dual-output comparison workflows. Rather than treating OpenWebUI only as a chat frontend, I extended it into a research-oriented routing and evaluation platform for collecting high-quality human preference signals under realistic usage conditions.

Technically, the project built on OpenWebUI's native Multi-Model Chats and Evaluation/Arena capabilities, and further designed two routing modes: Pinned Battle (user selects model A while the system injects a sampled competitor B) and full dual-output routing (all requests sample A/B pairs from a model pool). The routing design emphasized anonymous comparison, left-right position randomization, closed-loop voting, event-level logging schema, and bucketed experimentation to reduce bias and make collected preferences suitable for downstream router training.

I also studied how to connect online preference collection with offline router optimization under practical constraints such as quality-cost-latency trade-offs. The workflow incorporated ideas such as Fast Mode, replayable evaluation infrastructure, and pairwise preference aggregation inspired by Bradley-Terry style ranking, with the goal of training or calibrating routing strategies that achieve better Pareto performance in real deployments.

Fully Domestic Traditional Chinese Medicine Large Model

2025

This project aimed to build a fully domestic large model for Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) to support the full pipeline of cultivation, research, and clinical diagnosis. The system addressed key challenges in TCM AI, including dependence on foreign compute infrastructure, heterogeneous multimodal data, fragmented knowledge systems, and hallucination risks in clinical decision support.

My main contributions focused on knowledge graph construction and the RAG/GraphRAG pipeline. Using DeepSeek-R1-32B, I participated in organizing and structuring a large-scale TCM knowledge base covering 140,000 data entries and more than 300 classical medical books. I worked on connecting heterogeneous sources such as ancient texts, clinical knowledge, and domain-specific medical resources into a retrieval-augmented framework that could better support reasoning and evidence grounding.

Technically, the project combined a domestic computing stack with knowledge graph construction, graph-enhanced retrieval, and explainable reasoning for TCM scenarios. The resulting system supported clinically relevant tasks such as syndrome reasoning, formula recommendation, and evidence-supported treatment assistance, and was demonstrated through a working demo system.

Intelligent Monitoring of Rapeseed Across the Full Growth Cycle Based on 3D Point Clouds

2025 - 2026

This project focused on 3D point-cloud-based phenotyping and structural analysis of rapeseed across the full growth cycle, aiming to replace low-efficiency manual measurements with an automated and fine-grained plant monitoring pipeline. The system targeted challenging stages such as dense foliage, flowering, and pod development, where occlusion and complex geometry make accurate analysis difficult.

I independently carried out the core technical work of this project, including dataset construction, model design and training, and implementation of the full analysis pipeline. I built a dedicated full-growth-cycle rapeseed point cloud dataset containing 240 samples, which supported research on 3D reconstruction, organ-level parsing, and structural modeling across different developmental stages.

Technically, I trained and modified both PointNet++ and PointGroup-style models for semantic segmentation and instance segmentation of rapeseed organs, including leaves, stems, flowers, and siliques. I also explored a hybrid Point2Skeleton + Mamba framework for point-cloud skeletonization, combining geometric perception with long-range sequence modeling to better capture plant topology and growth patterns from raw 3D data.

The project formed a full-stack 3D plant analysis workflow covering data acquisition, denoising and alignment, organ-aware segmentation, and topology-oriented structural modeling. It was supported by both the Undergraduate Research Interest Program and the National College Student Innovation and Entrepreneurship Training Program, and was successfully completed. Related outcomes included a Provincial Third Prize in the College Student Innovation and Entrepreneurship Competition and three invention patents, including two as first author.

Remote Sensing Recognition and Segmentation for Wheat, Rice, and Rapeseed

2025

This project focused on crop recognition and parcel-level segmentation for wheat, rice, and rapeseed using multi-source remote sensing imagery. The technical pipeline combined 0.5 m high-resolution Jilin-1 satellite images with Sentinel-2 multispectral data to improve both spatial detail and spectral discrimination in agricultural land analysis.

The workflow included data preprocessing, remote sensing data organization, and model fine-tuning for semantic segmentation. I used SegFormer as the core segmentation model to perform crop identification and land parcel delineation, leveraging its transformer-based architecture for effective multi-scale feature extraction and robust segmentation performance across heterogeneous agricultural scenes.

Beyond model development, the project was integrated into a complete frontend-backend application workflow. The final system supported interactive visualization of original imagery and predicted parcel information through a curtain-style sliding comparison interface, making it easier to inspect segmentation quality and compare recognition results with the source remote sensing images.

Overall, this work gave me practical experience in remote sensing data fusion, semantic segmentation model fine-tuning, and the engineering of an end-to-end application that connects geospatial AI models with usable visual analysis tools.
